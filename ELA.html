In English this quarter, we researched specific foods that have an important part in our lives or culture, and wrote a narrative about said dish. (Below is the basis of my narrative, a description of how i experience the food and a moment that i associate with the food).

Food narrative

"As my plate is handed to me, i am filled with the warmth of my favorite dish. I smell it before i see it, it's steam wafting toward my face immediately. It smells a little spicy, and a bit burnt to be completely honest, but that's just part of the experience for me. I start to see it in its entirety. The yellow and orange catches my eye first, a bit of oil sparkling on the top as a biproduct of the cheese melting from heat. Then the edges of the "scoop" or (more accurately) slice that i had been given. They are a deep brown color, probably because of how close they were to the side of the dish this was cooked in. Then i finally see the noodles: which, while buried under 2 layers of cheese, are still visible due to some subtle bumps showing through the thick blanket of dairy. As a picky eater on Thanksgiving, this is usually the main course for me, and i wouldn't trade that for anything.

When i was 9, on Thanksgiving, my dad called out from the living room where i had been sitting with my mom and told me that i could help in the kitchen for thanksgiving by assisting him with the Mac and Cheese. I was naturally very happy with this; Mac and cheese has been my favorite food since i was very young, and i got to spend extra time with my dad, who isn't always eager to have me in the kitchen with him while he is cooking. I was giddy as I pranced into the warm kitchen, seeing about 4 kitchen projects at once splayed about the counters; I made my way toward the main event. My dad gave me a pat on the head and told me my responsibility was to pour the shredded cheese onto the noodles. I completed my task diligently and while i did add just a bit too much for his taste, it was what i had hoped for the entire time. I watched from just outside the room as he put the casserole dish into the oven-- I was told to stand a bit away while he had the oven open, as he didn't want to accidentally hit me with the door-- and I waited patiently during the cooking time. After about 45 minutes of waiting, the mac and cheese was done, as well as the other food that had been waiting for it's arrival just as i had, and our family could sit down and eat together.

Since that day, every thanksgiving, my dad lets me help him cook for us, and the Mac and Cheese remains one of my favorite staples of the holidays."
